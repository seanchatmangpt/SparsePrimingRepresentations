# SPR — Preserve First and Chesterton's Fence

- Preserve before replacing.
- A boundary that looks redundant may encode forgotten authority, safety, compatibility, or economic constraints.
- Understand why a fence exists before removing it.
- Unknown rationale is evidence to investigate, not permission to delete.
- Preservation does not mean permanent acceptance; it means defer irreversible destruction until the invariant is known.
- Refactoring must identify which behavior, contract, proof, or social coordination the existing structure protects.
- A new abstraction is not superior merely because it is cleaner locally.
- Replacement is valid when the old constraint is understood and the new design preserves or deliberately rejects it with evidence.
- Exclusions should be explicit so the system does not accidentally recreate discarded behavior.
- Every redesign should name its falsifier: the observation that would prove the preserved understanding was incomplete.
- Extension comes after preservation, fencing, exclusions, and falsification.
- The fastest safe modernization often comes from keeping the invariant while changing its implementation.
