# SPR — Design for Combinatorial Maximalism

- Preserve the maximum reversible lawful possibility space before irreversible selection.
- One failed edge is topology information, not evidence that the graph has failed.
- Explore broadly while consequences are cheap and reversible.
- Collapse options only when ontology, capability, authority, cost, or evidence requires selection.
- A decision should preserve future lawful substitutions whenever possible.
- Reversible diversity is inventory; premature convergence is waste.
- Canonical graphs dominate one-off projections because many artifacts can be regenerated from one admitted source.
- Generated artifacts are projections, not privileged editing surfaces.
- Failure should remove the narrowest invalid edge rather than erase adjacent possibilities.
- Admission converts possibility into a bounded lawful candidate.
- Actuation occurs only after the selected candidate crosses the authority boundary.
- The objective is not maximal complexity; it is maximal retained lawful optionality per unit of irreversible commitment.
