# SPR — Ontology-Driven Industry Closure

- Industry closure means an admitted capability graph can reconstruct the essential platform needed to operate an industry domain.
- Closure is broader than a vertical application; it captures reusable capabilities, policies, evidence, workflows, and projections.
- Public ontologies are preferred where they provide shared semantics and substitution across organizations and tools.
- Canonical graphs are sources of meaning; APIs, schemas, code, dashboards, infrastructure, and documents are projections.
- R2RML-style mappings connect relational state to semantic graphs without making storage layout the ontology.
- Generators manufacture projections from admitted semantic inputs.
- Formal admission proves structural or logical constraints before actuation where proof is available.
- Runtime systems execute the remaining empirical behavior and produce receipts.
- Closure requires correspondence across graph, query, generation, admission, runtime, actuation, receipt, replay, and release.
- A missing projection is usually an extension problem; a missing invariant is a closure problem.
- Substitution survival tests whether the capability is industry-level rather than vendor-shaped.
- Closure is achieved when the canonical model can lawfully regenerate multiple implementations while preserving meaning and evidence.
